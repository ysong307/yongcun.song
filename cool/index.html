COOL

Control, Optimization, and Learning

COOL connects systems, algorithms, and data.

The programme

Control, optimization, and learning for scientific systems.

COOL brings together mathematical analysis, numerical computation, and modern learning methods for problems governed by dynamics, partial differential equations, and data.

The programme studies how control, optimization, and learning can reinforce one another in methods that are structurally informed and computationally effective. It connects questions in scientific computing, PDE-constrained optimization, and scientific machine learning without treating those areas as separate silos.

Working principle Structure informs algorithms.

Models, objectives, numerical methods, and learning architectures are considered together—not in isolation.

Three connected pillars

Better decisions begin with mathematical structure.

COOL treats control, optimization, and learning as connected parts of one mathematical and computational problem. Models define the structure, optimization makes decisions computable, and learning extends what can be represented and solved.

01 / Control

Control

Decisions for systems governed by partial differential equations and dynamics, with particular attention to constrained and nonsmooth problems.

PDE-constrained optimization · Dynamical systems · State and control constraints

02 / Optimization

Optimization

Algorithms that exploit mathematical structure, including operator-splitting, primal-dual and ADMM-type methods, stochastic optimization, and proximal quasi-Newton ideas.

Structured algorithms · Nonsmooth problems · Large-scale computation

03 / Learning

Learning

Physics-informed, optimization-informed, and operator-learning methods for scientific problems, studied alongside approximation, generalization, convergence, and error questions.

Scientific machine learning · Operator learning · Mathematical foundations

Research approach

From models to methods—and back again.

Many scientific problems are neither purely computational nor purely data-driven. COOL treats the model, the optimization problem, the numerical algorithm, and the learning architecture as parts of one design problem.

01

Model

PDEs, dynamical systems, inverse problems, design, and simulation provide the scientific setting.

02

Formulate

Objectives and constraints define a control, inference, or optimization problem.

03

Compute

Numerical methods are designed around nonsmoothness, scale, and the structure of the system.

04

Learn & analyse

Learning models are developed and studied alongside approximation, convergence, generalization, and error questions.

Model → Formulate → Compute → Learn & analyse
